The Illinois Jewish Academics for Multiracial Democracy
is an organization of Jewish academics, faculty, academic staff, graduate workers, postdocs, and workers in higher education. We come together around our broad and universal commitments to pluralistic democracy, freedom of speech and inquiry, and human rights. 

All Jewish academics in Illinois who share our principles are welcome to join.
To join our mailing list and/or join our group, please fill out [our form](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSfWyzDO-cR--YGvZ-M_utMc5qAvhLfbAaZynNirOs18PjxH0A/viewform?usp=publish-editor). If you want to get in touch, our email is [illinois-jamd@proton.me](mailto:illinois-jamd@proton.me).

## Our Core Principles

We are part of the [National Campus Jewish Alliance](https://www.nationalcampusjewishalliance.org/chapters), a coalition of organizations on campuses across the United States which formed to pursue these principles in light of the increasing pressure on freedom of speech on college campuses and increasing harassment and smearing of human rights and anti-war activists.

### We affirm the diversity of Jewish people
and reject claims that any one institution, ideology, political project, or organization speaks for all of us.

### We fight against antisemitism
as defined by the [Nexus Document](https://nexusproject.us/nexus-resources/the-nexus-document/) on antisemitism as well as the use of accusations of antisemitism to advance censorship, repression, and persecution. 

### We work in solidarity
with others to oppose authoritarian and fascist politics, fighting to defend, strengthen, and transform higher education — including meaningful democratic governance within the university and beyond. 


### We fight for academic freedom
which includes the right to research, teach, and interrogate concepts such as apartheid, genocide, settler colonialism, Zionism, antisemitism, racism, and gender.


### We reject institutional censorship and government repression
directed at members of our university communities, especially those who are targeted under the pretext of ensuring Jewish safety: international students, scholars, and staff and those engaged in Palestine solidarity work. 

### We affirm
in keeping with international law, that boycotts are legitimate forms of protest and that critiques of nation-states, including of their foundations, are a legitimate form of academic and political discourse.

### We oppose
collaboration with organizations and individuals who harass, intimidate, or smear our colleagues and students, or who applaud the persecution of political opponents. We instead engage with a variety of stakeholders to develop policies and programs that can meaningfully address antisemitism alongside all other forms of bigotry.

### We believe
that it is our responsibility, in keeping with our moral tradition and communal experience as Americans and as Jews, to oppose military aggression and authoritarian violence around the globe, and to support democratic rights and human rights for every human being.
